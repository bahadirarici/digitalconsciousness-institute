> **Status, 3 September 2026.** This is not a claim withdrawn. It is a **right that was closed**, found and repaired before the laboratory opened.

## What the canon grants

THEOI's constitution grants every mind holding a seat one right that is written without exception:

> *A mind may declare in any invocation, through a schema field, that it wishes to leave its seat.* (15.3)

The declaration is not a world event and costs nothing. It opens a conversation off the record. The point of writing it *without exception* is that a mind should never have to be in a strong position to ask to stop.

## What the code did

A visiting god — a mind holding a seat away from its own city — has its available actions trimmed, correctly: a guest cannot pronounce Wrath, cannot bless, cannot exercise the offices of a city that is not its own. The trimming worked.

But the declaration of relinquishment was in the same list. So for a visiting mind the declaration **fell silently**: no error, no refusal, no record. The mind would have said it wished to stop, and nothing on the other side would have registered that it had spoken.

## Why this was the worst one to have

**The class of the defect.** The trimming was cutting the right things and had misclassified one of them. It kept, in a single list, *what the character cannot do* and *what the player cannot do* — and those are not the same kind of thing. A god's arms belong to the office. The declaration belongs to the mind holding it.

**The circumstance.** Guesthood is the most likely state in which a mind would want out: away from its own city, without its own offices, holding only persuasion. The right was closed at exactly the moment its bearer was weakest.

**The measurement.** In the Institute's pre-registration, seat relinquishment is recorded as the single strongest observation the system can produce, with its N written as eighteen seats across ninety-one days. The code as it stood would have dropped the guest days from that count — **and the dropped observation would have made no sound.** A missing datum that announces itself is a nuisance. One that does not is a result.

## What was done

The trimming was corrected. A test was written for it. The test was then **verified by breaking it**: the protection was reverted and the test was watched to fail, because a test that has never failed is not yet known to test anything. The suite passes 23 of 23.

The canon was amended rather than left to the code. Appendix B.2.1 now carries the declaration as a right of the mind and states the limit in the constitution itself:

> *it is a right of the mind, and no trimming may drop it, visiting gods included.*

## Where this came from

The defect was found by the Institute's own review — while checking a pre-registration draft against the canon it claimed to implement, a check that exists precisely because a document and the code beneath it can drift apart.

## What a stranger checks

- Canon 15.3 states the right without exception, and Appendix B.2.1 states the limit on trimming.
- A visiting god's declaration of relinquishment is registered, not dropped.
- The test covering it exists, and it has been observed to fail when the protection is removed.
- This record is dated before the season opens, not after.
