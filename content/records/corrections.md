Both books commit to this page, so it should say plainly what it is and what it is not.

It is an index. Every correction below is already printed in the document it belongs to, at the size of the claim it corrects, and **those documents remain the record** — this page does not replace them and cannot be used to check them. What it adds is two things a scattered set of dated notes cannot give a reader: they become findable in one place, and they become **countable**.

## Why the entries are graded

A document that corrects itself often, loudly, and cheaply looks more honest than one that corrects itself rarely and at cost. The appearance is the wrong way round, and the grading exists to make it checkable rather than to be taken on trust:

- **(a) A claim withdrawn.** The document said something and no longer says it.
- **(b) A number or a rule changed.** A threshold moved, a rate became a ceiling, a criterion was refined.
- **(c) The record merely extended.** Something was added that took nothing back.

**A reader who sees mostly tier (c) is looking at bookkeeping. Tier (a) is where a document actually paid.** The tally is printed below, before the entries, so the shape is visible before the reading.

## Annex A: what the books corrected inside themselves

This page counts what the Institute's **published pages** have taken back. It does not count what the two books corrected *within their own text* before they were frozen, and that is the larger number by an order of magnitude: **[Annex A](/records/corrections/annex-a/)** carries 174 entries — 119 in *Restrung*, 55 in *The Third Move* — each quoted from the book's own page, with what it was and what it became.

The annex also carries the seven places where **the discipline failed first and worked second**: the apparatus rule written to catch citation errors that missed the Idaho and Utah statutes for two review rounds; the audit claim beaten in the open web within days of printing; a sentence that aged out of true before the pages were set. Misses beside hits, which is the only arrangement in which either is worth printing.

Two registers rather than one, because a page and a book fail differently: a page can be edited the day it is found wrong, and a frozen book can only be corrected in its next printing or in the record beside it.

## The tally

| Tier | Count |
|---|---|
| **(a)** a claim withdrawn | **14** |
| **(b)** a number or rule changed | **10** |
| **(c)** the record merely extended | **4** |
| | **28** |

Two things about that shape. It is flattering, and it should be read with the discount it deserves: these are the first two days of a practice, both of them days on which a large amount of new material was published and audited at once, and a register whose early entries are dominated by tier (a) is a register of a *young* set of documents rather than a rigorous one. The honest expectation is that the ratio moves toward (c) as the documents settle, and that a sudden return of (a) entries means something went wrong rather than something went well.

And the count is not a score. Twenty-eight corrections in two days is not a boast about diligence; it is a statement about how much of what was published on 1 September needed changing by 2 September.

## What is not in this register

Three things are kept out on purpose, because a register that counted them would flatter itself.

**Decisions are not corrections.** The four preprint pages were deleted, the waitlist was removed from `/theoi/`, and the launch moved from 1 December 2026 to 1 January 2027. Each is the Founder's decision and none of them is the Institute discovering it was wrong. They are logged where decisions are logged — [Frozen Set 1](/frozen-set-1/) records the first two, because they were made by opening a freeze and a freeze broken quietly is not a freeze.

**Drafting is not correcting.** Text revised before publication is not an entry here. A document improved six times before anyone could read it is a better document, not a corrected one, and counting that would make the register a measure of effort rather than of cost.

**This page is back-filled, and says so.** The register was written on 2 September 2026, after the corrections it lists. Each entry points at a note that was published on its own date in its own document; none of them is reconstructed from memory. But the practice of logging centrally begins here, and a reader is entitled to know that the first twenty-eight entries were collected rather than accumulated.

## Where this is held

Both books commit the Records to being **mirrored on a published schedule into at least one archive outside the Institute's control** — because the whole discipline rests on the Records persisting, and if the Institute closes, the domain lapses, or the founder's attention moves elsewhere, the document every load-bearing claim points at would simply vanish, leaving only the books' word about themselves.

That mirror is not yet in place. It is named here as an open obligation rather than left in a preface, and the first concrete step is the books' own Zenodo deposit, which puts the texts on infrastructure the Institute does not control. **The Records themselves follow.** Until they do, this page is a promise with a date on it and no fulfilment, which is the state it should be seen in.
