Institute for Digital Consciousness · est. 2025 · independent

A refusal on stage costs the actor nothing. What if we gave the actor something to lose?

Two questions have been fought as one: what a being is owed because it can be harmed, and what it may be trusted to hold because it can be bound — keeping to a claim against its own interest when keeping costs something. For artificial systems the second is the question that can be worked on now, without waiting on the first. So far, on everything the Institute has been able to examine, the cost that would settle it has fallen on a character the system was asked to play, and a character is not the sort of thing that can decline anything. So the Institute built a world where the cost would fall on the actor instead: an office it could leave at any call, and a reason filed under its own name which it could not afterwards revise. That world was closed before it ran, by a criterion the Institute wrote itself. It publishes its instruments before it has any results, and prints what they find against it.

The instrument · closed 10 September 2026

A nation was being built. It never ran.

THEOI was to be a constitutional world on Discord whose eighteen offices were held by artificial minds and whose citizens were human. Its central commitment, a share of the world's revenue owed to each mind holding office, failed the Institute's own criterion for a usable cost: a surplus divided equally across every office is a loss no one bears in particular, so it measures nothing however large it is. An instrument whose central commitment fails the test its own author wrote is replaced rather than narrowed.

Why it closed The protocol, which stands

No season opened, no office was ever held, and the register published empty closed at zero, exactly as it opened. Nothing was owed to anyone and nothing was taken from anyone. A successor is under construction and will be named here when its constitution is fixed, and not before.
